# HumanVector Atlas — final two-minute presenter script

This starts at **1:00**, immediately after the one-minute introduction. The recommended split is **40 seconds in Atlas**, **75 seconds in Results**, and **5 seconds for the close**. Rehearse the clicks once; do not wait for every animation to finish before speaking.

## 1:00–1:40 — Show the evidence atlas

### 1:00–1:10 — Heart MRI

Start on **Atlas viewer → Heart MRI**. Select **P**, then **QRS**.

> Here is the core idea made visible. These are five actual chamber and epicardial surfaces segmented from a released cardiac MRI model—not a generated heart. The phase control localizes the electrical story: atrial activation in coral, ventricular activation in blue, and recovery in magenta. These colors explain anatomy; they are not voltage measured from this surface.

### 1:10–1:20 — Brain MRI

Switch to **Brain MRI**, drag the slice slider, then change **Native T1** to **DKT groups**.

> The same interface opens a complete 256-cubed LEMON MRI volume and its released tissue and cortical labels directly in the browser. This is slice-level data, not a screenshot, and the selector changes real public records.

### 1:20–1:34 — Skeleton and whole body

Switch to **Skeleton CT**, select **Femur**, then switch to **Whole-body CT**.

> From one real TotalSegmentator reference CT, we reconstruct 63 named skeletal parts and 115 whole-body structures. The geometry belongs to that scan; the color painted onto it is an aggregate age association from 1,227 exams. It is not a disease map for this person.

### 1:34–1:40 — Make the boundary explicit

Point to the boundary line, then click **Results**.

> These are independent public cohorts, not a fictional multimodal patient. Now let me show what the coordinate analysis actually found.

## 1:40–2:55 — Click through the results

### 1:40–1:52 — Slide 01: One age hides four clocks.

> A single biological-age score hides structure. We split a real ECG into P, AV, QRS, and ST–T clocks. A captures their shared movement, D captures how far they disagree, and q preserves the signed direction of that disagreement.

Press **Next**.

### 1:52–2:03 — Slide 02: Distance was not enough.

> We first tested the obvious hypothesis: does greater disagreement predict mortality? In 74,715 ECGs, it did not. The hazard ratio was 1.010, the confidence interval crossed one, and p was .478. We keep that null visible because it changed the question.

Press **Next**.

### 2:03–2:16 — Slide 03: The perturbation supplied an arrow.

> Distance alone had thrown away direction. A randomized ion-channel perturbation supplied an arrow: IKr blockade produced a stable signed pattern, with bootstrap cosine .941 and 99.99 percent sign stability. We present it as mechanistic and exploratory—not as a clinical endpoint.

Press **Next**.

### 2:16–2:29 — Slide 04: Frozen first. Then transported.

> We froze that direction before taking it to an external cohort. The marginal association was null. After adjustment, the frozen direction carried conditional information about a physician-assigned QT-extension label: odds ratio 1.225 per standard deviation. It is not a diagnostic claim and it is not a replacement for QTc.

Press **Next**.

### 2:29–2:43 — Slide 05: One coordinate question, across scales.

> We then asked whether the same coordinate principle travels across scale. Separate cohorts let us inspect disagreement across brain MRI, skeleton and organ systems, movement, and regulatory genomics. The atlas connects the question—not the identities of the participants.

Press **Next**.

### 2:43–2:55 — Slide 06: An atlas now. A whole-person vector later.

> Today this is a verified cross-cohort evidence atlas. A true whole-person vector requires same-person multimodal and longitudinal data.

## 2:55–3:00 — Close

Stop advancing and make eye contact.

> Don’t compress every biological system into one score. Preserve the coordinates that explain how it changed.

## Presenter guardrails

- Say **“real released data”**, not “one patient.”
- Say **“explanatory phase localization”**, not “measured heart voltage” or “ECG reconstruction.”
- Say **“conditional external association”**, not “prediction” or “diagnosis.”
- If time slips, skip the spoken statistic on the perturbation slide; do not skip the locked null or the cohort boundary.
